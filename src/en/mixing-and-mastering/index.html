What is a professional mix?

Mixing is the process of blending and processing every track of a song — vocals, beat and instruments — so each element sounds clear, balanced and intentional.

Mixing is decision-making: how upfront the vocal sits, how hard the kick and 808 hit, where each sound lives in the stereo field and which effects make the record memorable. I shape every element with EQ, compression, reverbs, delays, automation and creative effects so your music translates on any sound system.

In Latin and urban music, vocal mixing makes or breaks the song. That's why my Vocal Mix includes vocal editing, Melodyne tuning and vocal alignment on top of the advanced mix. Deep dive (in Spanish): what is mixing in a song.

What is mastering?

Mastering is the final step before release: it optimizes the finished mix for competitive loudness and consistent tone, so it sounds just as good on every platform and device.

If mixing decides how each element sounds, mastering decides how the whole song sounds next to everything else on your playlist: on earbuds, in the car, in a club or on the radio. On an EP or album, it also makes the tracks feel like one body of work.

With stem mastering I master from grouped stems (vocals, drums, bass, music), which gives more tonal and dynamic control than a single stereo file. More in my mastering guide (Spanish).

What's the difference between mixing and mastering?

Mixing works on the individual tracks and builds the sound of the song; mastering works on the finished mix and prepares it for release. You mix first, then master.

What makes a professional mix different from a generic one?

Judgment. A preset or an automatic mix applies the same settings to every song; a professional mix makes decisions for your song, your voice and your genre.

How loud should a master be for Spotify and other platforms?

Spotify and YouTube normalize playback to around −14 LUFS integrated, Apple Music to −16 LUFS and Deezer to −15 LUFS. That doesn't mean you must master to those values: a louder master simply won't play louder, only with less dynamics.

Default normalization by platform (subject to change). LUFS = Loudness Units relative to Full Scale; dBTP = decibels true peak.

Reference masters in reggaeton, trap and urban pop are usually louder, and that's an artistic call. What matters is that your song sounds dense, clear and distortion-free once the platform turns it down.

How to prepare and send your stems for mixing

For Mix or Full Mix/Mastering

  • Separate tracks, at least 44.1 kHz / 24-bit WAV.
  • No compression on the master channel.
  • Final level before export no higher than −6 dB.
  • Vocals on separate tracks: lead, backing, ad-libs…
  • Tracks without reverb, delay or compressors.

For Mastering Only

  • Instrumental as a WAV of at least 44.1 kHz / 24-bit.
  • Vocal on one WAV track of at least 44.1 kHz / 24-bit.
  • Uncompressed master channel and final level at −6 dB.

Upload everything to Google Drive, WeTransfer or similar and send the link to:
