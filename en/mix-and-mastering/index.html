Quick guide

What is mixing and mastering?

Mixing and mastering are the two final stages of producing a song. In mixing, every track (vocals, beat, instruments) is balanced and processed so each element is clear and intentional. In mastering, the finished stereo mix is optimized —loudness, tone, dynamics and peaks— so it sounds professional and consistent on every platform and device.

Mixing: shaping the record

Mixing is where the decisions that define your sound are made: how upfront the vocal sits, how hard the 808 hits, how the backing vocals breathe and where every sound lives in the stereo field. I use EQ, compression, saturation, automation, reverbs and delays to give the song clarity, punch and emotion.

In Latin and urban music the vocal mix is everything: tuning, de-essing, dynamic control and creative effects that turn a good take into a hit. Not sure what you need? Check the mixing and mastering services.

Mastering: the final quality check

Mastering is the bridge between your mix and the listener. It dials in tonal balance, loudness (LUFS), true peak and stereo image so your track stands next to the songs in your playlist and keeps its energy when Spotify or Apple Music normalize it.

On an EP or album, mastering also makes the songs feel like one body of work. See recommended levels in mastering for streaming.

Tech specs

Mastering for Spotify, Apple Music and YouTube: LUFS targets

Streaming platforms normalize playback loudness, so a louder master doesn't play louder — it plays at the same level with less dynamics. The key is a master that's competitive for your genre, with safe peaks (true peak ≤ -1 dBTP), that keeps its punch after normalization.

Reference normalization levels by platform (default settings, subject to change). LUFS = Loudness Units relative to Full Scale.
PlatformReference normalizationRecommended true peak
Spotify-14 LUFS integrated-1 dBTP (-2 dBTP if louder than -14 LUFS)
Apple Music-16 LUFS (Sound Check)-1 dBTP
YouTube / YouTube Music-14 LUFS-1 dBTP
Amazon Musicapprox. -14 LUFS-1 / -2 dBTP
TIDAL-14 LUFS-1 dBTP
Deezer-15 LUFS-1 dBTP

Does that mean you should master to -14 LUFS? Not necessarily. Reference masters in reggaeton, trap or urban pop are usually louder, and that's an artistic call. What matters is that the master sounds dense, clear and distortion-free once the platform turns it down. Want a second opinion on your current master? Send it over.

Checklist

How to prepare your stems for mixing and mastering

A clean export saves time and improves the result. Run through this list before you send your project.

Stuck exporting from FL Studio, Ableton, Logic or Pro Tools? Ask in the contact form and I'll help you get it right.

For mixing

  • 24-bit WAV at the session sample rate (44.1 or 48 kHz).
  • Every track starts at bar 1 and has the same length.
  • No master bus processing and no clipping on any track.
  • Separate vocals (lead, doubles, harmonies, ad-libs) with clear names.
  • Include BPM, key, rough mix and 1–3 references.

For mastering

  • Stereo mix as a 24-bit WAV, no limiter on the master bus.
  • Leave -6 to -3 dBFS of headroom on peaks.
  • Include the track order for EPs and albums.

FAQ

Mixing and mastering FAQ

What is mixing and mastering?

Mixing is the process of balancing and processing every track of a song (vocals, beat, instruments) with EQ, compression, effects and panning so everything sounds clear and hits hard. Mastering is the final step: it optimizes the finished stereo mix for loudness, tone and dynamics so the song sounds professional and consistent on Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube, radio, headphones or in the car.

What is the difference between mixing and mastering?

Mixing works with all the individual tracks and decides how each element sounds. Mastering works with the finished stereo file and prepares it for release: competitive loudness, safe true peak, tonal balance and consistency across songs. You mix first, then master. See the full comparison table.

How much does it cost to mix and master a song?

Pricing depends on the service (mixing only, mastering only or mix + master), the number of tracks and whether it's a single, EP or album. I send a fixed, no-obligation quote before any work starts: tell me about your project or email info@jmcastillo.es.

How long does mixing and mastering a single take?

Turnaround is agreed in the quote based on session complexity and availability. If you have a release date, mention it in your first message so delivery can be planned with enough margin for your distributor.

How should I send my stems for mixing?

Export every track or stem as a 24-bit WAV at the session sample rate (ideally 44.1 or 48 kHz), all starting at bar 1 with the same length and no master bus processing. Upload them to WeTransfer, Google Drive or Dropbox together with a reference track, the BPM and your rough mix. Read the full checklist.

How many revisions are included?

Included revisions are specified in your quote. The goal is for the song to sound the way you hear it in your head, so we work through your notes until the final version is signed off.

Do you work online with artists outside Spain?

Yes. The whole process is online: you receive mixes via link, send your notes and download the final masters. I work in English and Spanish with artists in the US, UK, Latin America, Europe and worldwide. Versión en español aquí.

How loud should my master be for Spotify?

Spotify normalizes playback to around -14 LUFS integrated and recommends a true peak of -1 dBTP (or -2 dBTP for masters louder than -14 LUFS). You don't need to master exactly to -14 LUFS: what matters is a master that sounds competitive for its genre and keeps its punch without distortion once normalized. See the platform table.

Can you master a song that someone else mixed?

Yes. You can book mastering only by sending your stereo mix as a 24-bit WAV with about -6 dBFS of headroom and no limiter on the master bus. If the mix has issues mastering can't fix, I'll tell you before work begins.

Which genres do you mix and master?

Mainly Latin and urban music (reggaeton, trap, R&B, dembow, afrobeats), pop and urban pop, as well as hip-hop, electronic and acoustic projects.
